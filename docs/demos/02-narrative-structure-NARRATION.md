# Narrative Structure — Ndotonic Author Series

## 1. Narrative Structure (9.58s)

Structure is not a cage. It is the shape that helps a reader understand what matters, why to keep turning pages, and why the ending feels earned.

## 2. Find The Engine (16.8s)

Find your book's engine. In fiction, it may be suspense or a character's change. In nonfiction, it may be an argument, a question, or a surprising revelation. Write one sentence that names the force pulling the reader from chapter to chapter.

## 3. Design The Ending Feeling (16.2s)

Decide the feeling you want to leave with the reader — hope, clarity, shock, resolve. Then list what must be understood, changed, or resolved for that ending to feel satisfying. Structure becomes clearer when you work backward from the landing.

## 4. Know Genre Expectations (17.3s)

Study the expectations of your genre. Notice what readers value in comparable books — pacing, twists, teaching style, emotional payoff. Then choose the distinctive voice, characters, ideas, or approach that make yours stand out without breaking the promise.

## 5. Map The Beats (17.71s)

Map a simple spine. Opening: show who wants what. Middle: raise pressure and force choices. Climax: the decisive confrontation or insight. Aftermath: show what changed. You can refine the map later — first you need a path you can walk.

## 6. Scenes That Advance (16.66s)

Every scene should change something: new information, a decision, a cost, or a shift in relationship. If a scene only restates what we already know, cut it or give it a turn. Structure lives in those turns, not in chapter count alone.

## 7. Fiction Versus Nonfiction (16.22s)

Fiction and nonfiction need different shapes, but both need tension and payoff. Fiction often follows desire under pressure. Nonfiction often follows a question under discovery. Choose the shape that serves the promise you named at the start.

## 8. Use Structure As A Guide (12.41s)

Use structure to help you begin and keep writing, not to trap you. You can revise or break a convention when it makes the book more powerful. The map is a tool. The draft teaches you what the map missed.

## 9. In Ndotonic Ink Studio (13.56s)

In Ndotonic Ink Studio, create chapter placeholders for your spine, then draft toward each beat. If a better order appears, reorder. Structure should serve the reader — and the tools should serve your revision.

## 10. Your Next Step (11.66s)

Your next step: write a one-page spine. Name the engine, the ending feeling, and three turns that must happen along the way. Keep it rough. Next up: building characters.
