# Cover and Description — Ndotonic Author Series

## 1. Cover and Description (8.04s)

Cover and description make one promise. They should tell the right reader what kind of book this is and why it matters.

## 2. Cover Essentials (12.74s)

For the cover, prioritize a clear title, readable type at thumbnail size, and one visual idea that matches the book's tone and category. Busy covers disappear in a marketplace grid.

## 3. Category Signals (13.42s)

Use category signals honestly. Romance readers, business readers, and memoir readers scan for different cues. Misleading imagery creates bad reviews. Promise what the pages actually deliver.

## 4. Description Hook (13.2s)

For the description, open with the reader's problem, desire, or an intriguing situation. Then state the benefit or the question that pulls them onward. The first two lines do most of the work.

## 5. Proof And Precision (12.79s)

Finish with proof and precision: who the book is for, what they will gain, and a clean call to begin reading. Ask a friend if the promise is clear in ten seconds. If not, rewrite.

## 6. Avoid Spoilers And Hype (11.78s)

Hint at conflict without spoiling the ending. Skip empty adjectives like groundbreaking or unforgettable unless you can show why. Specifics beat vague praise every time.

## 7. Thumbnail Readability (11.14s)

Check the cover at thumbnail size on your phone. If you cannot read the title, neither can buyers. Increase contrast and title weight until the name survives the shrink.

## 8. In Ndotonic (10.92s)

In Ndotonic, you can generate AI cover options, then judge them as a cold reader would. Keep the strongest promise. Pair the final cover with your description before you publish.

## 9. One Promise Check (12.77s)

Do a one-promise check. Cover and blurb should say the same book — same genre, same tone, same central tension. Mixed signals confuse the right reader and attract the wrong one.

## 10. Your Next Step (9.38s)
