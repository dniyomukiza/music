# Getting Started — Ndotonic Author Series

## 1. Getting Started (9.34s)

If you have never written a book before, you are in the right place. In three minutes, we turn a vague idea into the first pages of a real draft.

## 2. No Single Right Path (14.62s)

There is no single right way to start a book. Your process can be as individual as your story. What matters is that it helps you make steady progress instead of waiting for perfect conditions, perfect sentences, or the perfect mood.

## 3. Lower The Bar (16.54s)

When the blank page feels intimidating, lower the bar. Start with a few words, a memory, a question, a scene, or even a messy list. Material on the page gives you something to shape. Silence gives you nothing to revise.

## 4. Capture Ideas Anywhere (14.62s)

Make idea capture easy. Keep a notebook nearby, or use quick notes and voice memos. Write imperfectly. Capture the spark now, clean the language later, and never trust your memory to hold a good idea forever.

## 5. Pantser Or Plotter (16.39s)

Choose the approach that helps you move. A pantser discovers the book while drafting. A plotter plans characters, conflict, and the world first. Many authors combine both: a loose map for direction, then discovery on the page for voice.

## 6. Pick One Idea (13.99s)

If you have ten ideas, pick one. Ask which story you would still care about after a hard week. Commitment creates momentum. You can return to other ideas later. Right now, one clear direction is enough to begin.

## 7. Name The Promise (15.0s)

Before you draft chapters, write one plain sentence: this book follows someone who wants something, faces a real obstacle, and changes by the end. That sentence is your promise to the reader, and a compass when you get lost.

## 8. Build A Tiny Habit (16.08s)

Build a small daily writing habit. Five focused minutes count. Consistency makes a first draft possible because each session adds to the work already there. Protect a repeatable time, even if it is short, and treat showing up as the win.

## 9. A Simple Exercise (15.67s)

Here is a simple exercise. Write one short scene where your main character wants something specific. Put one obstacle in the way. End the scene when they make a choice. That choice is the seed of your next page, and often the start of your plot.

## 10. Draft Messy On Purpose (15.0s)

In a first draft, progress beats polish. Do not stop to perfect every sentence. Leave placeholders, skip ahead when stuck, and keep the story moving. You can revise later. You cannot revise a blank document.

## 11. Bring It Into Ndotonic (13.34s)

When you are ready to grow the draft, bring it into Ndotonic. Start from a story pitch, keep writing in Ink Studio, and use the editing tools when you want craft help — with you remaining in control of every change.

## 12. Your Next Step (15.14s)

Your next step is simple. Open a blank page, set a five-minute timer, and write anything related to your idea. When the timer ends, you are no longer starting from zero — you are mid-draft. Next up: narrative structure.
