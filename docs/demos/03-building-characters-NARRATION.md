# Building Characters — Ndotonic Author Series

Voice: `en-US-AvaMultilingualNeural` via edge-tts (-2%).

Duration: **2:13** (132.6s)

## 1. Building Characters (10.08s)

Readers stay engaged when they can follow how a person changes while meeting conflict, choices, and consequences. Characters are the heart of the story.

## 2. Go Beyond The Plot (13.68s)

Get to know your character beyond the plot. Write down what they want, fear, believe, and feel — and what motivates them when no one is watching. Public goals are useful. Private motives make decisions feel true.

## 3. Add Observable Details (15.24s)

Add observable details: clothing, body language, patterns of speech, a gesture, a favourite food, and the ways this person behaves around family or friends. Specifics make a character visible without long description dumps.

## 4. Private Character Sheet (14.71s)

Create a private character sheet. Explore memories, relationships, work, values, morals, and goals. Much of it may never appear on the page, but it will make choices feel inevitable instead of convenient.

## 5. Desire Versus Need (13.61s)

Separate desire from need. Desire is what they chase. Need is what they must learn or become. Conflict lives in the gap between those two. When desire and need clash, scenes gain heat.

## 6. Relationships As Pressure (14.62s)

Treat relationships as pressure systems. Allies enable. Rivals block. Loved ones raise the cost of every choice. A character alone can think. A character in relationship must reveal who they are.

## 7. Short Scene Under Pressure (13.42s)

Write a short scene of dialogue or action. Let a specific desire and fear shape their decision, then let that decision create conflict and move the plot forward. If the scene could fit any character, dig deeper.

## 8. Voice On The Page (11.5s)

Voice is character. Notice word choice, rhythm, what they notice first, and what they refuse to say. A consistent voice lets readers recognize a person even before you name them.

## 9. In Ndotonic (13.68s)

In Ndotonic, keep character notes beside your draft in Ink Studio. Write test scenes that force choices. When you want a second set of eyes on consistency, use AI review — and keep final judgment yourself.

## 10. Your Next Step (10.06s)

Your next step: write one page covering want, fear, need, and one pressure scene. Then draft that scene. Next up: developing plot.
